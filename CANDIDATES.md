# Candidates — One-Line Office Joke Laugh-Off

Judged locally against the published brief, criteria in order:
1. One-line office joke about work life. 2. Real laugh, sharp surprising
punchline. 3. Clear on one read. 4. Fresh, no office cliches. 5. Tight wording.

Field at build time: 1 other submission, source withheld, so no field text to
scan. All candidates below are original lines written in this session.
Themes avoided as cliche or already used in our earlier entries: reply-all
admin, birthday card, meeting-that-could-have-been-an-email, Monday, coffee.

## Winner

**W — I suggested removing our office suggestion box. It won employee of the month.**

- Office life: suggestion box and employee of the month are concrete workplace
  fixtures (1). Punchline is a sharp reversal: the box slated for removal is
  honoured as the best employee, landing in the final words (2). One read, no
  explanation needed (3). Suggestion-box premise is fresh, not a stock office
  gag (4). 77 characters, every word earns its place (5).

## Runners-up

- **A — I labeled my office fridge food 'evidence' — nobody touches it now, but HR wants a word.**
  Two turns (evidence, HR), clear and tight. Loses to W on freshness: fridge
  labelling is a known office-joke setup.
- **F — I posted in our urgent-issues channel. Three people reacted with eyes, and the building is still on fire.**
  Fresh eyes-reaction detail, strong escalation. Loses to W on tightness and
  on grounded office realism; the fire exaggeration softens the punchline.
- **C — The office plant died on my watch, so I replaced it with a plastic one — it just got promoted for never taking a day off.**
  Good promotion twist. Loses to W on economy; longer setup for one laugh.
- **B — I put 'do not reply-all' in my email signature. My boss replied-all to praise it.**
  Tight and sharp, but reply-all is a well-worn office theme. Loses on (4).
- **L — I replied 'sounds good' to the wrong thread, so now I'm organizing the retirement party for a man I've never met.**
  Specific and funny. Loses to W on punchline sharpness; the turn is gentler.
- **E — I named the office printer 'Kevin' so when it jams I can say Kevin's having a moment — HR says Kevin needs a performance plan.**
  Fresh Kevin twist, but the printer setup is a cliche and the line runs long.
- **I — My boss said 'we're a family here,' so I asked for an allowance — he gave me a deadline instead.**
  Clean twist on a familiar boss line. Loses on freshness.
- **J — I set my status to 'in a meeting' and went for coffee — the meeting found me there, holding two coffees.**
  Mild observation rather than a sharp punchline. Loses on (2).
- **D — My calendar says 'focus time,' so my coworkers schedule meetings in it to ask why I'm unavailable.**
  Clear but a mild, familiar complaint. Loses on (2) and (4).
- **G — I brought my own mug labeled 'not yours' — someone washed it, filled it, and left a thank-you note inside.**
  Warm, not a laugh. Loses on (2).
- **N — My desk chair squeaks in meetings, so now I only agree with ideas I can nod to silently.**
  Gentle and wordy. Loses on (2) and (5).
